Shared prompt
Create a full-bleed 5:7 portrait development-card illustration for a Renaissance trade game, readable at 150×210px. Depict {scene} in the {gem} environment at tier {level}. Use realistic painterly artwork, rich natural materials, atmospheric depth, and broad, clearly defined forms. Match the antique-gold arabesque deck ornament and polished gemstone assets with restrained decorative detail and a thin muted border. Keep one strong focal point outside the reserved overlay areas, leaving quiet areas at the top corners and bottom-left for game symbols. Flat front view of the card; no surrounding scene, cast shadow, title, lettering, numbers, logos, or watermark. Avoid tiny details and visual clutter.

Scene diversity
Treat gem features and scene examples as a menu, not a checklist. Choose one primary subject and at most two supporting features. Give each card a distinct silhouette and composition, recognizable at 150×210px. Vary the subject, viewpoint, spatial arrangement, and lighting between cards of the same gem and tier. Preserve the shared painting style, border, and overlay clearance. Card-specific details take precedence over generic scene examples and lighting suggestions.

Gem variants
Onyx — Dark cave. Obsidian walls, glowing crystal veins, an underground lake, deep blue-black shadows, and warm torchlight. Hidden wealth, rarity, and secrecy; keep important forms readable within the darkness.
Sapphire — Sea. Deep blue water, merchant ships, coastal trade routes, sea cliffs, and sunlight on waves. Maritime commerce and exploration, with bright skies and distant harbors.
Emerald — Forest. Massive ancient trees, emerald foliage, moss-covered ruins, forest paths, and sunbeams through the canopy. Natural resources, growth, and abundance, with architecture integrated into the woodland.
Ruby — Fire. Volcanic settlements, lava rivers, blacksmith workshops, molten metal, and glowing furnaces. Craftsmanship, industry, and power, with orange-red light against dark stone.
Diamond — High mountains. Sharp snow-covered peaks, glaciers, mountain passes, alpine fortresses, and bright crisp light. Prestige, purity, and nobility, with ivory, silver, and pale-blue tones.

Tier variants
Level 1 — Modest resource-gathering locations, simple structures, intimate scale, and one clear activity.
Level 2 — Established settlements and trade centers, substantial architecture, and a wider view of organized commerce.
Level 3 — Exceptional engineering, craftsmanship, natural grandeur, or commercial importance, with epic scale and a strong sense of prosperity. Palaces and panoramic cities are optional. Convey importance through composition and scale rather than extra fine detail.
